# REALCITY Part 7 — Reference Game Study

Date: 2026-10-18  
Purpose: derive general, original open-world design principles for REALCITY. This is a public-reference study, not a claim of access to proprietary source code or a direct inspection of the games' internal engines.

## Sources and what they can prove

1. **Grand Theft Auto V — official Rockstar page:** https://www.rockstargames.com/gta-v  
2. **Rockstar Newswire — GTA V announcement:** https://www.rockstargames.com/newswire/article/o349k552544449/grand-theft-auto-vi-official-announcement.html  
   The announcement describes Los Santos alongside hills, countryside and beaches, supporting the high-level principle of a city embedded in a larger regional environment. It does not document the engine's implementation.
3. **Cyberpunk 2077 — official site:** https://www.cyberpunk.net/  
4. **CD PROJEKT RED — official launch announcement:** https://www.cdprojekt.com/en/media/news/cyberpunk-2077-is-out-now/  
   The public material frames Night City as a large, dense urban setting with many places and people. This supports study of layered urban composition and activity density, not claims about exact rendering algorithms.
5. **Gangstar Vegas — official Gameloft page:** https://www.gameloft.com/gangstar-vegas/  
6. **Gangstar Vegas — official game site:** https://gangstar-vegas.com/  
   These describe an open-world city, driving activities and a broad vehicle roster. This is relevant to the target mobile open-world genre. It does not establish the game's internal mobile optimization techniques.
7. **Gangstar Vegas — official Gameloft gameplay trailer:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tAklhBPPPPE  
   Public gameplay footage can be used to observe framing, activity variety, vehicle silhouettes and city composition. A trailer is curated footage, not a reliable performance benchmark.
8. **Three.js color management manual:** https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html  
   This is implementation guidance for correct color-space handling and texture annotations, not a game reference.

**Research boundary:** no proprietary game assets, maps, source code, textures or animations are copied. The study extracts general principles and turns them into original REALCITY tasks. Detailed internal systems, exact frame budgets, AI algorithms and proprietary art pipelines are not inferable from marketing pages or trailers.

| Player animation and movement | Movement feels intentional when the body, facing, speed and camera agree; a model alone is not enough. | Public gameplay footage from the official GTA V and Gangstar Vegas channels/pages; no claim about their internal animation rigs. | Clear silhouette, responsive turning, suitable transition timing, foot contact and camera follow reduce the sense of sliding. | REALCITY has a remote GLB load path and procedural fallback. Clip selection is name-based and the runtime view has not been inspected in this audit. | Approve the actual asset and license; record detected clips/bones; add only states supported by verified clips; test turn/start/stop/jump and vehicle transitions before expanding the controller. | Medium; one player rig plus a small number of active clips. | In a recorded running session, idle/walk/run/jump visibly change correctly, facing tracks movement, no severe foot sliding/snapping, and fallback works offline. |
| Pedestrian variety and pathing | A small population feels more alive when pedestrians use plausible routes, turn at corners and avoid one another instead of merely wandering. | Public game footage and official descriptions establish visible world activity, but do not disclose the reference games' exact AI algorithms. | Consistent sidewalk use, varied silhouettes, spacing and small pauses communicate purpose. | REALCITY has 22 procedural people and a simple adjacent-waypoint selector; no verified designated crossings or long-session runtime test. | Convert the sidewalk grid to explicit nodes/edges with corner transitions, bounded rerouting, progress timeouts, spacing checks and a fixed population cap. Add appearance variety only after navigation works. | Medium; throttle distant AI/animation and pool objects. | A 5-minute run shows no persistent stuck loops, building cut-through, lane wandering, crowd freeze or unbounded NPC growth. |
| Vehicles and driving | Driving feels coherent when visual steering, movement direction, braking and camera behavior agree. | Official Gangstar Vegas pages describe open-world driving and varied vehicles: https://www.gameloft.com/gangstar-vegas/ ; https://gangstar-vegas.com/ | Distinct silhouettes and stable controls make vehicles readable; traffic gives streets a sense of scale. | REALCITY uses procedural car geometry and custom update logic with Rapier collision support; realistic wheel suspension is not established. | First fix lane and collision behavior; then add visible steering/wheel rotation and restrained body response tied to actual velocity/steering, keeping the existing physics model. | Medium; avoid a second physics engine and unnecessary per-wheel simulation. | Repeated drive course passes acceleration, braking, reverse, turn, collision, service, enter/exit and reload without teleporting, persistent overlap or broken save state. |
| Activity and interactions | An open world needs a small number of reliable, meaningful interactions rather than many decorative prompts. | Official Gangstar Vegas pages describe roaming and driving activities; this is a high-level genre reference, not a copied feature specification. | Nearby objects respond predictably, controls are clear, and entering/exiting spaces preserves orientation and state. | REALCITY exposes cars, doors and kiosks; one interior is built at remote coordinates and switched visible/invisible. The user-facing prompts require runtime validation. | Stabilize one enterable interior and one kiosk loop; use explicit interaction types, proximity tests, safe spawn/exit points and save/reload handling. | Low–medium. | 20 consecutive enter/exit cycles plus refresh/reload work; no falling through geometry, stranded player or false interaction label. |

## Implementation order derived from the study

1. **Correctness first:** sidewalk coordinates, asset provenance, honest telemetry, clean startup and a repeatable build.
2. **Readability second:** district silhouettes, ground-floor frontage, aligned junctions and material separation.
3. **Movement third:** verify the player rig and clips, then validate pedestrian path traversal and vehicle transitions.
4. **Performance fourth:** establish same-device baseline, then batch static geometry, share materials, limit shadows/texture work and throttle distant AI only where measurements justify it.
5. **Content expansion last:** add more interiors, props and NPC variants only after current interactions and regression tests pass.

## Limits and non-claims

- No claim is made that REALCITY can match AAA games in asset budget, world scale or simulation depth.
- No proprietary technical details are attributed to Rockstar, CD PROJEKT RED or Gameloft.
- No asset, map, texture or animation is copied from the reference games.
- A READY Vercel deployment, official marketing page, or gameplay trailer is not proof that REALCITY's WebGL runtime or OPPO A5 Pro performance passes.
